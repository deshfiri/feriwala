Sourcing module

Product Sourcing Groups: staff-declared sets of catalogue products that can fulfil one another's orders. This mechanism is now legacy history; allocation no longer reads it. The module also holds the staff-only queries behind the "Suppliers" section of a product.

Legacy. The route comment in routes/web.php says groups "are kept as history only and no longer affect allocation". OrderItem marks sourcing_group_id, sourcing_canonical_product_id and sourcing_canonical_variant_id as "never written now". Allocation offers only sources for the exact ordered product and variation (Order\Queries\AllocationSourceCandidates). Do not build new behaviour on groups. The related ProductSourceLink (Order module) is likewise marked legacy.

Purpose

A group said "these catalogue products are commercially interchangeable when fulfilling an order" (for example one supplier's "Regular Pants" and the warehouse's "Men's Regular Pants"). It was a fulfilment concept, never a browsing category and never inferred from names or SKUs. It was later replaced by the rule "one central product has one BPC and many Supplier Offers": Supplier Offers are the one record of a supplier holding a product. Staff-only; implements D25 (supplier confidentiality) context. See Supplier for offers and Order for allocation.

Key terms

Sourcing group
A named set (code, name_en, name_bn) of products declared fulfilment-equivalent.
Canonical product
The member flagged is_canonical; the others map onto it. Only one active canonical per group (partial unique index).
Variant mapping
An explicit row saying a member's variation fulfils a canonical variation. Membership alone is never enough: Black/M does not match Blue/L.
Product source link
Older cross-catalogue assertion (Order module, product_source_links); inactive history.
Supplier offer
The live record of a supplier holding a product (Supplier module); what allocation uses today.

Group-level audit entries are all filed against the group, so its history reads as one timeline.

States & lifecycle

No transition enum. A group is active or inactive (is_active); memberships and mappings are active or removed (SourcingMappingStatus), one-way by convention: removal records who, when and why and the row is kept.

Product suppliers section

ProductSuppliersSection::forProduct() lists a product's supplier offers (and forSupplier() a supplier's), returning null to anyone who may not ProductSupplierPolicy::canView(). The Supplier Base Price (supplier_rate) appears only if the viewer has supplier_pricing.view; it is confidential (D25). ProductLinkSummaries builds staff-recognition cards (image, BPC, barcode, variation summary, counts of active supplier offers and active-warehouse stock items) for the product pickers; it never goes into a client, partner or storefront payload.

Permissions & scoping

Module sourcing_group: view, create, edit, archive (archive toggles active). Product Manager holds view/create/edit/archive; Admin holds view only. Product suppliers use product_supplier.view|create|edit; assigning or editing an offer's price also needs supplier_pricing.edit, and seeing prices needs supplier_pricing.view. ProductSupplierPolicy refuses anyone with an account membership. Groups are staff-only, not account data, so there is no self-scoping.

Money, ledger & audit effects

The group tables hold no money. The supplier-offer price (Supplier Base Price) edited from ProductSupplierController is a Money value parsed by DecimalAmount::parse and handled by ChangeSupplierBasePrice (Supplier module, reason required). Audit actions above; sourcing_group.* names are used without a module-prefixed enum.

Configuration

None.

Rules, invariants & gotchas

  • Groups no longer drive allocation, but the tables, screens and permissions remain, and ManageProducts::assertPermanentlyDeletable() and ForceDeleteProduct still check/deactivate group memberships and mappings.
  • Nothing is deleted: removal rows stay as the explanation for past allocations.
  • A product with variations must be mapped variation by variation.
  • product_supplier_assignments (migration 2026_10_31_100000) exists, but no application code references it; assignment now creates a SupplierOffer with origin admin_assignment through Supplier\Actions\AssignProductToSupplier.

How to…

  1. Read why an old order line was fulfilled from another product: open the group at admin.sourcing-groups.show and the order line's snapshot columns on OrderItem.
  2. Add a rule to group membership: change ManageSourcingGroups, add a refusal to SourcingGroupRefused, and extend tests/Feature/Sourcing/SourcingGroupScreenTest.php. Prefer not to; the feature is legacy.
  3. Assign a supplier to a product: use the product's Suppliers section (product-suppliers.store), not groups.
