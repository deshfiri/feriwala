Wallet and ledger

The wallet holds an account's money. Every change to it goes through one service, WalletService, which writes an immutable ledger entry in the same database transaction. This page covers wallets, typed balances (platform credit and withdrawable), the ledger, holds and reservations, deposit rules and low-balance enforcement, plus the shared Concurrency and Idempotency helpers that financial code leans on.

Rule. Nothing writes a wallet balance column outside App\Domain\Wallet\WalletService. Ledger entries are never updated or deleted: a mistake is answered by a new correcting entry. Every posting runs under a row lock inside a transaction and is idempotent on a key. Money is an exact decimal Taka string (Money), never a float, never poisha. See the Money API reference.

Purpose

Every active business account has exactly one wallet (§23, §24). It is where a partner's earnings land (sales, commission, COD collection), where top-ups and deposits are held, where service charges, returns and damage costs are debited, and where withdrawals are reserved and paid from. The wallet belongs to the business account, not to the person who clicked (D1, D23).

Who uses it:

  • Client / Partner reads their own wallet and statement, tops it up through a payment gateway, and withdraws from it (see Withdrawal).
  • Admin staff view any wallet, post manual adjustments, reverse postings, convert platform credit to withdrawable money, and set deposit rules.
  • The system credits settled gateway payments, posts referral rewards, charges services, and runs the daily balance check and ledger integrity check.

Specification sections: §23 (wallet and ledger), §23.1 (transaction types), §23.2 (immutable ledger entries), §23.3 (transaction statuses), §24.1 to §24.4 (deposit, minimum balance, restrictions, refundability), §26.3 (what a payment is for), §28.1 (reconciliation), §31.3 (self-scoping), §36.1 (money and concurrency). Decisions: D4 and D26 (currency, flat Taka), D17 (refundability), D24 (referral rewards), D25 (Supplier wallets are a separate domain), D36 (Partner-gateway receivable debit). The code calls the typed-balance decision "D-new"; it has no numbered entry in requirements/04-decisions.md that I verified.

Supplier wallets are a different model (App\Domain\Supplier\Models\SupplierWallet, SupplierWalletService) and are covered on the Supplier page.

Key terms

Wallet
One row per business account (wallets.business_account_id is unique). Stores primitive buckets only; "usable" and "withdrawable" are computed in Wallet, never stored.
Total
Everything the account holds. Every other bucket is a claim against it.
Platform credit
Money spendable inside Banij and never withdrawable: top-ups, deposits, referral and joining rewards, promotional credit. The only stored split (wallets.platform_credit).
Withdrawable
Everything that is not platform credit: sales earnings, commission, COD collection. Derived as total - platform_credit.
Reserved
Money set aside against a coming charge (a withdrawal request is the main case). Records how much of it was platform credit (reserved_platform_credit).
Hold
Money frozen under review. Records its platform-credit part (hold_platform_credit).
Pending
A bucket for money credited but not yet cleared. See the gotcha below: nothing in the code writes it today.
Minimum balance, required deposit
Obligations captured from a deposit rule. Both stand against the wallet; neither can be withdrawn. The deposit can be spent on services only when deposit_usable_for_charges is true.
Claim
A WalletTransaction whose status is the life of a reservation or hold: pending (reserved) or on_hold. Released (cancelled) or captured (settled) exactly once.
Ledger entry
The frozen record of value that moved. One row per kind of money moved; written once.
Posting context
PostingContext: source, description, idempotency key, reason, internal note, actor, payment id and so on.
Obligation (captured)
A WalletDepositObligation row: the figures from the deposit rule as they were when captured. Restrictions read these, not the live rule.
Restriction stage
One of the graded §24.3 consequences of a short wallet (notice, services restricted, website setup paused, website disabled, account restricted, account disabled).

The formulas (do not shortcut them)

From .ai/rules/wallet.md and Wallet.php:

  • Available to spend (usableBalance()) = total - reserved - hold - pending - minimum_balance - (required_deposit if the deposit may not cover charges), floored at 0.
  • Platform credit available = min(max(0, platform_credit - reserved_pc - hold_pc), usableBalance()).
  • Withdrawable available (availableForWithdrawal()) = max(0, (total - platform_credit) - reserved_w - hold_w - pending - minimum_balance - required_deposit), where reserved_w = reserved - reserved_platform_credit and likewise for hold.

Never compute withdrawable as total - platform_credit: that is a holding, and it still contains reserved, held and pending money. Pending, minimum balance and required deposit are untyped and always stand against withdrawable money first. A spend takes platform credit first, then withdrawable; debit(..., $only) and allocateSpend($amount, $only) take the whole amount from one kind or fail. Supplier wallets use their own variant (see the Supplier page).

Flow 1: a credit or debit (WalletService::post())

  1. Resolve direction: the type's own (LedgerTransactionType::direction()), else the context's, else the caller's. Correction types return null and make the caller say.
  2. assertPostable(): amount positive, currency equals the wallet's (D4: no conversion), a reason when requiresReason() (every correction and conversion), an actor when requiresActor() (manual adjustment and conversions).
  3. A credit needs a kind: the one passed, else creditBalanceType(); a type that maps to none is refused (balanceTypeRequired).
  4. Fast path: if a transaction with this idempotency_key exists, return it.
  5. Open a database transaction and re-read the wallet with lockForUpdate().
  6. For a debit, re-check usableBalance() inside the lock (a check before the lock proves nothing) and split the amount with allocateSpend(): platform credit first unless a kind is demanded.
  7. Create the settled WalletTransaction, update total and platform_credit, write one ledger entry per kind moved (keys key:platform_credit and key:withdrawable when it splits), then append a WalletTransactionEvent.
  8. After commit, for a real credit (not a conversion), dispatch WalletCredited.
  9. A UniqueConstraintViolationException (two identical commands raced) is caught; the winner's transaction is returned.

Flow 2: reserve, release, capture

  1. reserve() (status pending, bucket reserved) or hold() (status on_hold, bucket hold) locks the wallet, checks usableBalance(), splits the claim (a WithdrawalDebit claim may take only withdrawable money), raises the bucket and its platform-credit counter, and writes an event. No ledger entry: no value moved.
  2. release() locks the claim, requires canTransitionTo(cancelled) (otherwise alreadySettled), lowers the bucket by exactly the remembered split.
  3. capture() does the same, but also lowers total and platform_credit and writes the ledger entry (key = claim key + :capture), because only now has value left.

Flow 3: a gateway top-up

  1. Erp\WalletTopUpController::store (or mobile WalletTopUpController::store) parses the amount with DecimalAmount::parse(), then PlanWalletTopUp and RecordTopUpPayment create a draft Payment.
  2. A gateway session opens (see Billing); the payer pays.
  3. SettlePayment verifies with the gateway under a lock and marks the payment paid. Its applyPurpose() calls CreditSettledPayment for wallet_deposit and wallet_top_up only.
  4. The credit posts as deposit_credit or top_up_credit (platform credit) with key payment:{payment reference}, so IPN, browser return, reconciliation sweep and manual retry all land on one posting.
  5. If the account has no wallet, nothing is credited, a critical line goes to the wallet log channel and the payment is flagged wallet_credit_failed_at. An admin clears it with admin.payments.wallet-credit, which is idempotent on the same key.
  6. After commit, WalletCredited triggers ReleasePartnerReceivablesOnWalletCredit, which lets waiting Partner-gateway order receivables collect (D36; see Billing).

Flow 4: correction, reversal, manual adjustment

  1. CorrectLedgerEntry::reverse() posts the opposite movement of the same amount, pointing at the original through corrects_ledger_entry_id, with key reversal:{original entry reference}. The type is commission_reversal for a commission credit, referral_reward_reversal for referral and joining rewards, otherwise manual_adjustment.
  2. A credit's reversal is taken from the kind it came in as and is refused if that kind is gone; it is never paid out of the other kind. A debit's reversal returns to the kind it was spent from.
  3. reverseTransaction() reverses every ledger entry of a transaction (a spend that used both kinds wrote two).
  4. adjust() is a person's decision: reason and actor required, a credit must name its kind. Both write an audit row wallet.{ledger type} flagged sensitive.

Flow 5: platform credit to withdrawable

ConvertWalletBalance checks WalletPolicy::canConvertBalance() again inside the action, then calls convertToWithdrawable(): a required idempotency key, one lock, amount must not exceed platformCreditAvailable(), platform_credit falls by the amount (total unchanged), and two entries (balance_conversion_debit on platform credit, balance_conversion_credit on withdrawable) share a conversion_reference. Keys are wallet-conversion:{wallet public id}:{caller key}:debit and :credit. There is no reverse. Audit: wallet.balance_converted, sensitive.

Flow 6: deposit obligation and the daily balance check

  1. On activation, package change or service setup, CaptureDepositObligation resolves the rule for the account (ResolveDepositRule) and writes the captured figures to wallets and a wallet_deposit_obligations row. Capturing the same figures twice writes nothing new and never moves a deadline.
  2. SweepWalletBalances (scheduled 02:30) runs per wallet, restore first, then enforce. RestoreWalletServices lifts only restrictions this mechanism placed and returns the account to the status it had, and only when the wallet is healthy again.
  3. EvaluateWalletBalance sets balance_state. The grace deadline is stamped once, when the shortfall begins, and cleared when the account is back above the line.
  4. EnforceBalanceRules: send the notice (WalletBalanceLow) for low or critical; while the grace period runs, do nothing more; after it, apply each authorised stage in severity order, once. Audit actions wallet.restriction_applied and wallet.restriction_lifted.
  5. One wallet's exception is logged and counted; the sweep carries on.

Flow 7: ledger integrity

VerifyLedgerIntegrity (scheduled 03:00) checks, per wallet: credits minus debits equals total (balance_mismatch); the platform-credit entries net to platform_credit (platform_credit_mismatch); reserved_platform_credit and hold_platform_credit equal the sum of open claims (*_mismatch); each entry satisfies before + movement = after (entry_does_not_balance); and each entry's balance_before equals the previous one's balance_after (broken_chain). It never repairs anything and logs one critical entry to the wallet channel per sweep.

Self-scoping is structural. Rules, invariants & gotchas

Gotcha: the typed figures in toBalances(). In the array and the UI, platform_credit and withdrawable are what can be spent or withdrawn now (platformCreditAvailable() and availableForWithdrawal()). The holdings, before any claim, are platform_credit_held and withdrawable_held. Mixing them up is the classic way to show a withdrawal button for money that is reserved.

Gotcha: idempotency does not compare the payload. WalletService returns the existing transaction for a known key whatever amount the retry carries. Keys must therefore be derived from the thing being paid (a payment reference, a refund id, a commission id), never reused across different intents. IdempotencyService (below) does fingerprint bodies; the wallet's database key does not.

Gotcha: "pending" means two things. A reservation's transaction status is pending; the wallets.pending bucket ("credited but not cleared") is a different thing. I found no code in app/ that writes wallets.pending or wallets.cod_receivable; they are zero from OpenWallet and only read and displayed. Do not assume either is maintained.

  • Debits check the usable balance, withdrawals check the withdrawable balance. WalletService::debit/reserve test usableBalance(). RequestAccountWithdrawal additionally tests availableForWithdrawal() under the same lock, because the required deposit stays out of a withdrawal's reach even when it can cover service charges.
  • A debit is never overdrawn. A negative available balance is refused (WalletOperationRefused::insufficientBalance), before anything is written.
  • A wallet is never created on demand by a payment. CreditSettledPayment flags the payment instead. (Referral payout calls OpenWallet for a beneficiary, which does create one.)
  • Reversal of a credit may be impossible. If the kind it came in as has been spent, the reversal is refused. Referral reversals turn this into a "reversal owed" state that is retried.
  • Derived balances are floored at zero so "minus two hundred taka to spend" is never shown; the shortfall figures say the same thing the right way up.
  • A conversion is one-way. There is no platform-credit-from-withdrawable path.
  • Capture exists only for claims. settleClaim() treats a claim as hold when its status is on_hold, otherwise as reserved. Do not call release or capture on a settled posting; the state machine refuses it.
  • Cross-currency postings fail loudly (D4). There is no exchange-rate accounting on the ledger.
  • Plain DB::table('ledger_entries')->update() fails thanks to the trigger; do not "fix" data that way. Post a correction.
  • The balance sweep restores only what it placed (fromLowBalance() scope) and returns the account to the status recorded on the restriction, not to "active".
  • Verification runs after commit and is wrapped. In SettlePayment, a failed wallet credit never rolls back the payment; it is logged and flagged for an admin.

Shared helpers: DistributedLock and IdempotencyService

ClassBehaviour
App\Support\Concurrency\DistributedLockNamed Redis locks (key prefixed lock:). run($key, $callback, $ttlSeconds = 10, $waitSeconds = 5) blocks and throws LockTimeout; attempt() runs only if free and otherwise returns null; isHeld(). It asserts the cache store implements LockProvider and throws RuntimeException otherwise, so a non-Redis store cannot silently leave money unguarded.
App\Support\Idempotency\IdempotencyServiceCache-backed, 24 hour window (DEFAULT_TTL 86400). execute($key, $payload, $callback) runs once; a replay with the same key and body returns the stored value (IdempotentOutcome::replayed true); the same key with a different body throws IdempotencyKeyReused. The fingerprint is sha256 of the recursively key-sorted JSON (lists keep their order). A concurrent duplicate waits on a lock (idempotency:{key}, 30s ttl, 10s wait).

Rule. A distributed lock is coordination, not correctness. Money and stock still need a database transaction with row locking; the lock reduces contention and the database keeps the invariant true (§36.1). Durable idempotency for money is a unique database key (wallet_transactions.idempotency_key, ledger_entries.idempotency_key, payments.idempotency_key); IdempotencyService covers replay windows for external requests.

Both helpers are used well beyond the wallet (settlement, order, inventory, storefront replay, referral plan opening). Search app/ for DistributedLock and IdempotencyService before adding a third pattern.

How to…

Add a new ledger transaction type

  1. Add the case to app/Domain/Wallet/Enums/LedgerTransactionType.php. Keep the value at or under 40 characters (the column is string(40)).
  2. Add it to direction() (or leave it null if it is a correction), to creditBalanceType() if it is a credit with a default kind, and to label(). Every match is total, so the compiler will point at the rest.
  3. Decide requiresReason() / requiresActor() (they follow isCorrection() and isConversion()).
  4. Post it with WalletService::credit/debit and a PostingContext whose key is derived from the thing being paid. Wrap the whole business step in one transaction.
  5. Update tests/Feature/Wallet/TransactionTypeTest.php and add a service-level test.

Charge an account for a service from its wallet

  1. Ask DepositGuard::assert() (or permits() for a screen) with the charge, so the account is not pushed below its obligation.
  2. Call WalletService::debit() with the right ledger type; it re-checks under the lock. Use reserve() then capture() when the charge is confirmed later.
  3. Catch WalletOperationRefused and return its message; never catch it to retry with a smaller amount.

Add a field to deposit rules

  1. Migration on deposit_rules, wallet_deposit_obligations and, if the wallet reads it directly, wallets.
  2. Casts and docblock on DepositRule and WalletDepositObligation; copy it in CaptureDepositObligation::handle().
  3. Include it in the snapshot that ManageDepositRules writes to DepositRuleChange, the controller validation in Admin\DepositRuleController, and resources/js/pages/admin/deposit-rules.tsx. Add strings to lang/en and lang/bn.

Add a restriction stage

  1. Add the case, severity() (leave gaps of 10), label() and tone() in WalletRestrictionStage.
  2. Add a switch column on the rule and the obligation, and map it in EnforceBalanceRules::authorisedStages().
  3. Make whatever the stage takes away actually check WalletRestriction::standing(); the enum alone enforces nothing.

Check a wallet against its ledger by hand

  1. Run VerifyLedgerIntegrity::problemsFor($wallet) from a test or a one-off script; it is read-only.
  2. If it reports a problem, do not edit rows. Find the cause, then post a correction with CorrectLedgerEntry.
