Architecture

The system map: what Feriwala does for the business, who uses which surface, how the code is layered, and what happens to a request on each of the four entry paths (web, mobile API, storefront API, webhook). Read this first, then Conventions.

What Feriwala is

Feriwala is a centralized ERP for wholesale and dropshipping in Bangladesh. It is not a shop. The public Feriwala site has no catalog, no prices, no cart and no checkout (spec §1, requirements/00-understanding.md); the login screen is the landing page (Route::get('/') redirects to login). All real work happens behind login.

The business model in one page

  • Feriwala (the platform) owns the central catalog and the central stock pool. Only admins create products; partners select from the catalog (§11, §12).
  • Partners are the businesses that register, pass KYC, pick a package, pay the combined registration + package fee, and become Active. A partner can then buy in bulk (wholesale, for their own stock) and/or resell through dropshipping (publish catalog products to their own storefront; the customer's order is fulfilled by Feriwala).
  • Suppliers sell goods to Feriwala. They are a separate account domain (D25) with their own login, KYC, portal, wallet and payables. Suppliers list products, set confidential rates, hold offers and stock, and are paid through payables and withdrawals.
  • Storefronts are each partner's public website. Each Owner has one independently installed storefront built from a master template (D32). The storefront reads catalog/stock/prices from the ERP and writes orders and customers back through the frozen Storefront API. See Storefront app.
  • Customers of a storefront have no ERP account. They exist as website customers and pay for orders through a payment gateway or choose cash on delivery.
  • Couriers (Steadfast is the implemented driver) deliver orders; cash-on-delivery is tracked and reconciled separately from online payments.

Money flow and merchant of record

For dropshipping, Feriwala is the merchant of record (D12, non-negotiable 7): the customer pays a Feriwala-controlled gateway or selects COD; Feriwala verifies the payment, controls inventory, fulfilment, courier and COD reconciliation; the partner's earning (commission) is calculated once the order is eligible and credited to the partner's wallet; the partner withdraws from the ERP. "Retail margin" is a commission calculation base, not a separate money path.

D36 amends D12 for one case. D12 stands unchanged as Model A (Platform gateway). D36 (2026-11-02) adds Model B: where Admin allows it, a partner may collect a storefront customer's payment into the partner's own gateway account. For that transaction the partner is the payment recipient and Feriwala recovers its platform receivable from the partner's wallet. The payment direction is frozen on the payment when it is handed to a gateway. Read Decisions before touching payment code.

The single account structure

The most unusual thing about the project. Three decisions fit together (D1, D23, D25):

ConceptModel / enumAnswers
Login identityApp\Models\User, identity_statusMay this person sign in at all? Gates everything, admin included.
Commercial workspaceApp\Domain\Account\Models\BusinessAccount, AccountStatus (the 22 §5.3 statuses)May this business trade? Gates the ERP: wallet, orders, websites.
Staff of a businessAccountMembership, AccountInvitationA member belongs to the owner's account and does no KYC or payment of their own.
Platform authorityRoles and permissions (app/Domain/Access, PlatformRole, PermissionCatalogue)What a Feriwala staff member may administer.
SupplierApp\Domain\Supplier\Models\Supplier, guard supplier, provider suppliersA wholly separate identity; never converts to or from a User.
  • There is no "customer account" vs "partner account" and no upgrade path. One BusinessAccount moves through a status lifecycle; once activated it can do dropshipping, wholesale, or both from one panel, one KYC, one wallet.
  • A user owns at most one business account (database-enforced). There is no account switcher and no account segment in URLs: the account is resolved from the membership (App\Concerns\ResolvesBusinessAccount).
  • Platform staff reach permitted admin routes without owning a business account. A suspended identity loses everything; a suspended business loses the ERP for owner and members, but a staff member who also owns it keeps admin.
  • Keep three concepts apart in code: a status (lifecycle), a package entitlement (what was bought, entitled:staff_limit middleware, App\Domain\Package\Entitlements) and a permission (what a staff member may administer).

How modules depend on each other

There is no tooling that enforces a dependency graph; the rule is the Domain README's: cross-domain calls go through Actions or Events, not by rewriting another domain's models. In practice the dependencies run roughly from foundations upward:

Support, Concerns  (no domain knowledge)
  Access, Audit, Settings, Location, Bank, Address, Notification, Sms, Storage
    Account, Kyc, Package            (identity, activation funnel)
      Billing  (payments, invoices, fees, gateways)
        Wallet (wallet, ledger, balances)   Tax
          Catalog, Inventory, Sourcing, Supplier, ContentLibrary
            Wholesale, Dropshipping/Website, ExternalStore
              Order  (unified order management), Courier, Cases, Damage
                Commission, Referral, Withdrawal, Payout, Settlement

The arrows are approximate and one-directional by intent. Real examples: Wallet\Actions\CreditSettledPayment takes a Billing\Models\Payment and credits through Wallet\WalletService; Wallet\Actions\CorrectLedgerEntry records into Audit through RecordAuditLog. When the arrow would have to point downward, publish an Event (for instance Wallet\Events\WalletCredited, fired after commit) rather than importing the upstream class.

The Actions pattern

Business logic lives in Actions: one class, one public method, usually handle(). Controllers validate, authorize, delegate and respond. Models hold relationships, casts and scopes, never workflow.

// app/Domain/Wallet/Actions/CreditSettledPayment.php (abridged)
class CreditSettledPayment
{
    public function __construct(
        protected WalletService $wallet,
        protected LogManager $log,
    ) {}

    public function handle(Payment $payment): ?WalletTransaction
    {
        // ... decide whether this payment is wallet money at all ...
        return $this->wallet->credit($wallet, $type, $payment->amount, new PostingContext(
            source: 'payment',
            description: $payment->purpose->label().' - '.$payment->reference,
            idempotencyKey: 'payment:'.$payment->reference,
            paymentId: $payment->id,
        ));
    }
}
  • Actions receive typed arguments: a Money (never a raw number or decimal string) and DTOs from Data/. What a person typed is parsed at the HTTP boundary with DecimalAmount::parse().
  • Queries/ are read models and report queries, including self-scoping. Jobs/ carry queued work; Events/ and Listeners/ are how modules react to each other.
  • A shared infrastructure service that is not one operation can be a Service or a root-level class (WalletService is "the only thing that moves money in a wallet"; Actions call it).

Request lifecycles

1. A web request (ERP, admin, supplier)

  1. Kernel and web group. bootstrap/app.php prepends ApplyConfiguredSessionLifetime (it must run before StartSession), then the framework web middleware (cookies, session, CSRF), then appends, in order: HandleAppearance, DefaultReasonUnlessSecurity, EnsureIdentityHasPlatformAccess (global identity gate, signs out a suspended login), SetLocale, TrackAuthenticatedSession, HandleInertiaRequests, AddLinkHeadersForPreloadedAssets.
  2. Route middleware. ERP: auth (and often verified), business.activated, noindex, optionally entitled:<feature>. Admin: auth, noindex, two-factor, with RequirePassword on sensitive actions. Supplier: auth:supplier, supplier.operational.
  3. Controller. Resolves the account from the membership (ResolvesBusinessAccount), validates (a Form Request, or an inline $request->validate() with DecimalAmountRule for money), authorizes (policy, Gate, or the permission name module.action).
  4. Action runs the work in a transaction where it mutates money or stock.
  5. Response. Inertia::render('wallet/top-up', [...]) for pages (money passed as Money::jsonSerialize()), or a redirect with flash data. A 403 on catalog or inventory admin routes renders a branded Inertia refusal page (bootstrap/app.php).

2. A mobile API request

  1. routes/api.php mounts routes/mobile.php under mobile/v1 with mobile.locale and throttle:mobile. No session, no CSRF.
  2. Token routes add auth:sanctum and mobile.access:user (or supplier), which refuses a token held by the wrong kind of login and revokes the token of a suspended one.
  3. Business routes sit inside mobile.activated; pre-activation areas are registered outside it.
  4. A controller extending MobileController validates with a Form Request, calls the same Actions and Queries as the web app, and returns an API Resource (envelope data, links, meta). Details: Mobile API.

3. A storefront API request

  1. routes/api.php prefix storefront/v1, name prefix storefront.v1.. Stateless.
  2. storefront.log writes every call to api_logs, refused ones included.
  3. storefront.auth (AuthenticateStorefrontRequest) requires HTTPS, a non-revoked credential, a timestamp within ±300 s, a matching signature compared in constant time, and a nonce not seen for 600 s. Every failure is the same 401 to the caller with a precise reason in the log. The credential is the tenancy boundary: no endpoint accepts a website from the caller.
  4. storefront.host checks a stated storefront host belongs to that website; storefront.rate:read|write|other applies the credential's budget; storefront.scope:<scope> requires the endpoint's scope; writes also pass storefront.idempotent (Idempotency-Key, same key answered once with the same bytes).
  5. The controller (app/Http/Controllers/Api/Storefront/V1) delegates to Actions. There is deliberately no wallet, ledger, KYC, commission or account surface. The contract is frozen at v1 (requirements/05-storefront-api-contract.md).

4. A webhook

  1. Routes are in routes/web.php, CSRF-exempt (bootstrap/app.php: webhooks/payment/*, webhooks/stores/*, webhooks/courier/*), throttled (payment-webhooks, store-webhooks, 240,1 for Steadfast).
  2. The controller authenticates the message first (gateway signature, store signature over the raw body, or the bearer token for the courier), before looking anything up.
  3. It writes the message down (RecordPaymentLog), queues the work (SettleGatewayNotification) and acknowledges. Every outcome that is not a signature failure gets the identical Acknowledged. answer so the endpoint cannot be used to probe which references exist.
  4. The job asks the gateway directly what happened (a notification only says which payment to look at), then settles through the Billing and Wallet Actions, idempotently.
  5. The payer's browser coming back from a gateway uses separate checkout/return-style POST routes that run without the web group (GatewayReturnController); they settle nothing and 303 to a signed-in GET page.

Infrastructure

  • PostgreSQL 18 and Redis 8 run natively in WSL in development (D5). Docker and Sail are not a development path; compose.yaml is a production-parity reference only.
  • Redis carries cache, sessions, queues, rate limiting and locks on four separate databases, with locks isolated so a cache flush cannot drop a lock guarding a financial operation. Never switch session, cache or queue to file, sync or database, even temporarily: the app must stay stateless and horizontally scalable (§40, D10).
  • Heavy work is queued: SMS, email, exports, sync, settlement, reconciliation. Scheduled tasks are in routes/console.php. See Operations and Commands.
  • Real-time nudges use Laravel Echo over a private channel per person (routes/channels.php, NotificationsChanged::channelFor()); the socket only tells the page to reload.
  • Tests run in a testing schema in the same database. See Testing.
